## Article 1: Explainer: What are 'large language models'?

**Source:** Reuters  
**Date:** 2025-11-08T14:23:00Z  
**URL:** https://www.reuters.com/technology/explainer-what-are-large-language-models-2025-11-08/  
**Category:** Tech  
**Credibility Score:** 9  
**Relevance Score:** 10  

Large language models (LLMs) are a type of artificial intelligence system that uses neural network architectures with billions of parameters to understand and generate human-like text. They are trained on massive text corpora from the internet, books, and articles. By predicting the probability of successive words, they can compose coherent sentences, translate languages, write code, and even generate creative content. Examples include OpenAI's GPT series, Google's PaLM, and Meta's LLaMA. The underlying technology relies on transformer architectures, introduced in 2017, which use attention mechanisms to model relationships between words in a sequence. Recent advancements have scaled models to trillions of parameters, improving performance on tasks like question answering and summarization. However, LLMs can sometimes produce plausible but incorrect or biased outputs, a phenomenon known as hallucination. They also require significant computing resources and raise ethical concerns about misuse, privacy, and data bias. Researchers are exploring techniques to make LLMs more efficient, interpretable, and aligned with human values, such as fine-tuning with reinforcement learning from human feedback.
